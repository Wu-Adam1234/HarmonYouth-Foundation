How a show runs

Showing up, on repeat

Most homes only see entertainment once in a while. We wanted it on the calendar every month, so residents start expecting it.

01Booking

A home reaches out

An activities coordinator gets in touch, or we follow up with a home we already visit, and lock a date.

02Lineup

We show up and play

Five to ten students, 30 to 45 minutes, solo pieces and a few group ones — piano, strings, voice, clarinet.

03After

We follow up

Photos go out, certificates and service letters get signed, and the next home usually books off the back of them.

Programs

Two programs, one reason

Student musicians from around Calgary perform on a set schedule, not as a one-off concert. Nobody has to commit to every date — you sign up for what works, and you get a real audience instead of a panel scoring you out of ten.

Members 3D print and assemble devices from the Makers Making Change open-source library, reviewed by occupational therapists. Finished devices go to people in our community at little to no cost. You don’t need a printer — build meetings need hands more than hardware.

Any skill level you’re comfortable playing in front of people. We add you to the roster and check in before each show to see who can make it, so signing up never locks you into a date.

Both programs in full

Built to keep coming back — same homes, same faces, every month.
