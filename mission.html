Our mission

Students showing up for people who don't always get thought of first.

HarmonYouth Foundation runs two programs. We play live music at senior homes on a set schedule, and we 3D print and assemble assistive devices and adapted toys for people with disabilities. Same reason behind both.

Why we exist

Two programs, one reason.

Most senior homes only bring in entertainment once in a while. A holiday concert, maybe a choir that visits once a year. We wanted something that shows up on the calendar every month, so residents actually start looking forward to it.

We also wanted to give student musicians somewhere to play after their last recital or exam is done. Nobody has to commit to every single date. You sign up for whatever works for you, and you get a real audience instead of a panel scoring you out of ten.

Music is how we started, but it isn't the whole picture. A lot of the same students are good with their hands and have access to a 3D printer, so we added a second program: building assistive devices and adapted toys for people with disabilities. Different skill set, same reason for doing it.

Program one

Music at senior homes

Student musicians from around Calgary perform at senior homes on a set schedule, not as a one-off concert. Each show runs about 30 to 45 minutes: piano, strings, voice, whatever people bring, in whatever common room the home gives us.

How a show comes together

The same four steps, every show.

01

Someone reaches out

Usually it's an activities coordinator getting in touch, or us following up with a home we already visit, to lock down a date.

02

We check who's free

Everyone on our list gets asked if they can make that date. Whoever says yes, plus a few more, that's the lineup.

03

We show up and play

Usually 30 to 45 minutes, a mix of solo pieces and a few group ones, in whatever common room the home gives us.

04

We follow up

If people are okay with it, we post a few photos afterward. It's usually what convinces the next home, or the next musician, to give us a shot.

Program two

Build: assistive devices and adapted toys

Music was never the whole plan. HarmonYouth members 3D print and assemble assistive devices and adapted toys for people with disabilities, through Makers Making Change, a program of the Neil Squire Society.

Join a build meeting Upcoming build meets Visit Makers Making Change

A lot of assistive technology is either expensive or hard to get hold of, even though the designs to build it are often free. Makers Making Change keeps an open-source library of assistive devices and adapted toys, reviewed by occupational therapists and disability professionals, that anyone with a 3D printer and some patience can build.

We print the parts, then get together to assemble them, the same way we get together to rehearse and perform. Finished devices and toys go to people with disabilities in our community who could use them, at little to no cost.

It's a different kind of showing up than a piano recital, but it's the same idea: students doing something useful for people who don't always get thought of first.

How a build meeting works

From design file to finished device.

01

A request comes in

Through Makers Making Change, someone with a disability, a family member, or a disability professional identifies a device or adapted toy that would help.

02

We print the parts

We pull the design from the open-source library and 3D print the pieces ahead of the next meeting.

03

We assemble together

Members meet up to put the pieces together, wire switches, and test that everything actually works before it goes anywhere.

04

It goes to the person who needs it

The finished device or toy gets delivered, at little to no cost, to the person or family who requested it.

Want in?

You don't need to own a 3D printer

Build meetings need people to assemble, test, and problem-solve, not just print. If you'd like to join one, or you know someone who could use a device or adapted toy, reach out through Get Involved. Dates are on the Build meets page.

Volunteer hour certificates at every 4 hours. Performances and build meetings both count toward the same total, and we can write a volunteer service letter on request.
