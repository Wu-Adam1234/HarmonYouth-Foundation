Meet the team

The four of us, for now

HarmonYouth Foundation is started and run by students. Here’s who’s behind it.

Marketing and outreach

Vienna Lu — Marketing and Outreach Lead

Vienna runs the outreach side of HarmonYouth: our Instagram, promotional flyers, and helping organize performance rosters and build meetings.

Founder

From the person who started it

I’m Adam. I started piano when I was young, and this year I finished Level 10 through the RCM program, on top of regular school here in Calgary. At some point I started caring more about playing for people than playing for a grade.

The first time I went to a senior home, it wasn’t part of anything official — I just asked if I could play for an hour. People who hadn’t said much the whole visit were humming along by the end. That stuck with me more than any exam result ever has.

I started HarmonYouth because I didn’t want that to be something I did once and forgot about. I wanted it to be an actual schedule people could count on, and I wanted other musicians to get to feel what that afternoon felt like too.

More recently we added a second side to HarmonYouth: 3D printing and assembling assistive devices and adapted toys for people with disabilities, through Makers Making Change. Different skill set, same reason for doing it.

AdamFounder, HarmonYouth Foundation
Co-founder

Co-founded with someone who plays just as seriously

I’m Hanry. I’m RCM Level 10 on piano, and I’ve played clarinet for five years.

I co-founded HarmonYouth because I wanted our shows to actually run on a set schedule, with more than just two of us showing up each time.

It’s not supposed to be random pop-up performances. The idea is fixed dates we commit to, so we’re not just showing up unannounced one day.

If you have any questions, reach out to us.

HanryCo-Founder, HarmonYouth Foundation
Co-founder

Co-founded by someone who’d rather play for a room than for a grade

I’m Arthur. I started cello when I was young, and music has been a big part of my life ever since. What I enjoy most about it isn’t the practising, and it isn’t the run-up to a performance. It’s sharing music with other people and seeing what it does to a room.

At some point I started caring less about playing for a grade and more about playing for people. The first time I played at a senior home, it wasn’t part of any organization or event — I just asked if I could come in and play for about an hour.

That afternoon has stayed with me longer than any mark I’ve been handed. I co-founded HarmonYouth because I didn’t want it to be something I did once and moved on from. I wanted to keep sharing music with people regularly, and give other young musicians the chance to do the same.

Music isn’t only about performing well, at least not to me. It’s something I actually enjoy, and being able to share that with other people is what makes it worth doing.

Arthur WuCo-Founder, HarmonYouth Foundation
